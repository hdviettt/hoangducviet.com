Homepage hero · four treatments

Half the hero is column you never used

Measured on the live page at 1440: the hero is 485px tall and its content stops at x=702 inside a column that runs to 1233. That is 531px empty, 52% of the column, and it holds at every desktop width (57% at 1920, 39% at 1024).

The cause is not missing content. It is three nested caps, each smaller than the last: a 1026px shell, a container capped at 576px, and an h1 set at 32px that only needs 238px of it. blog.google's h1 is 60px. M3 display-large is 57px. labs.google runs to 120px.

My first read was that the gap exists because the type is too small, and that scaling it would close it. Measurement says no. With the name at 56px, option A still leaves 45% of the column empty; option D, at 72px, leaves 41%. "Hoang Duc Viet" is fourteen characters. At 72px that is about 500px of ink in a 954px column, and no defensible type size closes a gap that size.

So the four options are really two answers. A and D make the left half better and accept that a single column does not fill a wide screen, which is a legitimate editorial position and the one most Google text pages take. B and C put something in the right half and take the empty column to zero. The figures in the table below are measured live, in this page, at your current window width.

A

Scale to the column

blog.google · deepmind.google

Nothing is added. The 576px cap comes off the heading, the name runs at 40 to 56px against the viewport, and the bio keeps a reading measure of its own. This does not close the void: measured live above, it goes from 52% to 45%. What it does is make the hero look deliberate rather than cramped, and it is the only option that costs nothing to maintain.

For Smallest change by far and nothing new to maintain. This is what a Google editorial hero is: big type, one column, nothing beside it. A 32px name in a 1026px column is the part that reads as an accident; a 56px one does not.
Against It does not fix the empty column, it accepts it. 45% is still empty afterwards. If the void is what bothers you, only B and C answer it.

Portrait right

the portfolio convention

The photo leaves the text flow and becomes a real image: 340px square at a 24px radius, filling the right half. Text sits left at a comfortable measure. The two halves balance.

For Uses the whole column with content that already exists. A face at 340px is a different proposition from a face at 104px: it reads as a person, not an avatar.
Against The most conventional of the four, and it leans the page toward "portfolio" rather than "someone who writes". Google does not do this anywhere, because Google is not a person.

C

Panel right

product surfaces

The right half becomes a live panel on the tonal surface: what you are doing now, and the two most recent things you published. It is the only option that makes the top of the page carry information rather than identity.

For Answers the question a visitor actually has, which is "is this person active and what are they doing". Uses the surface ramp that now works correctly in both themes.
Against The recent-posts half duplicates the feed that is already further down the same page. And it is a panel you have to keep current: a stale "Currently" is worse than none.

Full statement

labs.google · M3 display-large

The name runs at 48 to 72px across the whole column and the role sits under it at 22 to 28px with the slash in the brand blue. The photo shrinks to 46px and joins the socials row, where it is a byline rather than a portrait. The bio drops below at a reading measure.

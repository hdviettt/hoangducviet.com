Feed row · three treatments

The same eight posts, three ways

Real titles, real dates, the real series block, at the real type sizes and the real column width. Hover the rows. Tab through them. Flip to dark.

The series is the case that decides this. Six standalone posts and one nine-part collection have to live in the same list, and the treatment that only looks good with flat rows is not a treatment.

A

Ruled list

what the site has today

A date column, a title, a hairline between. Hover changes the title colour and nothing else, which the measurement says is the correct pair for a ruled row: across blog.google and fonts.google.com, no element that gains a hover plate also carries a rule.

For Dense. Nine parts of a series fit without the page getting long. The date column gives every row the same left edge, which is what makes it scannable.
Against It is the least Google-like of the three. No Google index surface uses rules between rows. It reads editorial, closer to a newspaper index than to a product.

B

Card list

fonts.google.com

No rules. Each row is a 16px-radius card on a tonal surface and the gap between cards does the separating. Hover is the M3 state layer plus a radius response, which is the pattern the plate was actually designed for. The series becomes one larger card holding its parts, which is the only treatment here that gives the collection a visible container.

For The most Google-like of the three, and the only one where hover can do something. The series reads as a unit rather than as a row that happens to be taller.
Against Heavier. Eight cards is a lot of surface on a page that currently has none, and it pushes the list longer. Risks looking like a dashboard rather than a body of writing.

C

Whitespace list

google.com/search · news.google.com

No rules and no cards. The date sits above the title as a quiet label, the title carries the row at 21px, and 30px of space does the separating. Hover underlines, which is what a Google search result does. This is what news.google.com actually renders: the probe found zero ruled rows on it.

For The lightest and the most confident. Nothing is drawn that is not content. Titles get bigger because they no longer share a line with a date column.
Against Loses the date column's shared left edge, so the list scans less like an index. Nine series parts have no container at all and can drift into the posts around them.

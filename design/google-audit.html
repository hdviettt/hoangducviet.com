The twelve findings

01

A global letter-spacing of 0.5px that Google uses nowhere

Highest impact

Your globals.css sets body { letter-spacing: 0.5px }, and because tracking inherits, it lands on nearly every element on the site. Measured on your homepage, 0.5px appears on the name, the role line, the bio, the nav, the section headings and the feed rows.

At your 11.7px UI text, 0.5px is 0.043em. The equivalent Google token is --letter-spacing-base: 0.01188em on deepmind.google, about a quarter of that. On ai.google.dev, news.google.com and fonts.google.com, every single measured text run came back at exactly 0.

This is the single biggest reason the site reads as "close to Google but not Google". Loose tracking on small text is a 2014 Roboto habit. Google Sans Flex is drawn to be set solid.

02

A Material 3 type scale that exists in your CSS and is used zero times

Highest impact

globals.css defines all fifteen M3 type roles, from .md-display-large to .md-label-small, a hundred lines of correct sizes and line heights. A search across every public component returns zero uses. What the pages actually use is 112 one-off arbitrary sizes spanning 24 distinct values.

I expected this to show up as more type variation per page than Google has. It does not, and it is worth being straight about that: your homepage renders 17 distinct type specifications, blog.google renders 13, and ai.google.dev renders 27. On raw variety you are in the middle of the Google pack. Google is also perfectly happy to run one size at several line heights, 14px carries eight different leadings on ai.google.dev.

What separates them is not how many values there are. It is what kind of values they are.

04

The fluid root font size fights the 8dp grid

Structural

Your root is clamp(12.8px, calc(var(--shell-max) * 0.0140351), 16px). At 1440 it resolves to 14.4px. Everything on the site is sized in rem, so every spacing value inherits that scale and lands off the grid.

Fourteen distinct gap values were measured on your homepage, none of them whole numbers. blog.google renders six, all of them from {2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24}, with 24 used 32 times.

The clearest symptom is in your own documentation. globals.css declares .article-content { font-size: 1rem; line-height: 1.6 } and your CLAUDE.md describes it as "post typography (16px / 1.6 body)". The browser renders it at 14.4px. The stylesheet and the page disagree by 10% and have done for as long as the fluid root has been there, because 1rem stopped meaning 16px and nothing in the file says so.

The downstream effect on type is worse than on spacing. Your rendered sizes are 9.79, 11.7, 13.5, 14.4, 17.1, 17.28, 20.7, 28.8. Google's, on every property with a fixed root, are whole: 12, 13, 14, 16, 18, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36. Fractional sizes give fractional line heights, which is how you end up with 13.5px carrying four different leadings on one page.

This is the one recommendation you should push back on if you disagree. The fluid root is a deliberate decision with a long comment explaining it, and it does solve a real problem, which is that a fixed design rendered at 1265 and at 1920 is two different densities. Google does use fluid scaling on marketing surfaces: labs.google runs a 10px root and vw-derived display type.

The part that is not defensible is applying it to reading text. Every Google property that is mostly words, blog.google included, pins the root: 16px there, 18px on blog.google. Body text does not scale with the window at Google. Only display type does.

The fix

Keep the fluid idea, move it off the root. Pin html at 16px and drive only display-tier type from clamp() against the viewport. Spacing then lands on whole pixels, reading text stays 16px at every width, and the hero still scales.

Your site separates almost everything with a 1px rule: feed rows, work blocks, the stack panel, card edges. Those rules are drawn in outline or outline-variant. Both are lighter than Google's lightest boundary colour.

There is no strong-hairline option in the system at all, which is why the pages can read as washed out at a glance despite the type being nearly black.

The other half of the colour finding: Google tints its surfaces toward the primary hue, and yours are neutral grey.

The fix

High

I drove a real mouse over every interactive element on your homepage and diffed computed styles before and after. Every hover changed colour and nothing else. The outline colour moved with it, but only because outline-color resolves to currentColor. No background, no surface, no radius, no transform, no elevation.

The same probe on blog.google returns background-color: transparent → rgb(248, 249, 250). A grey plate appears under the thing you are pointing at. That is the Google idiom, and it is the M3 state layer: the "on" colour at 8% over the component, 10% on focus, 12% on press. You already implement it correctly in .md-btn::before, but that class is used almost entirely in the admin, not on the public site.

10

Skeletons are almost invisible, and only two routes have one

Medium

Your skeletons are the best-built thing in this report. They mirror the real layout in the real containers at the real widths, with deterministic row widths so they do not reshuffle between renders. Two problems, both small.

First, the colour. The bars are bg-muted, which resolves to #f9fafb against a #ffffff page. That is 1.03:1. The skeleton is very nearly invisible, so the loading state reads as a blank page rather than as a page arriving.

Second, the animation. animate-pulse is a 2s opacity fade on a bar you can barely see. Google's loading grey is #f1f3f4 or #e8eaed, and the motion is a shimmer sweep, which reads as progress rather than as breathing.

Now · #f9fafb, 1.03:1, pulse

#f1f3f4 with a shimmer sweep

Coverage is the other half. Only / and /posts have a loading.tsx. /about, /work, /work/[slug], /posts/[postSlug] and /collection/[seriesSlug] have none, so those routes hold a blank frame until the server responds.

What the loading probe actually found. I put a 6x CPU throttle and a 180kbps pipe on fonts.google.com, news.google.com, aistudio.google.com and blog.google, and logged every CSS animation running at any point during the load. Not one of them presented a skeleton. The only loading affordance that appeared was on blog.google, and it was a circular progress indicator: an SVG running spin 1.5s linear infinite with a second dash 1.7s ease-in-out infinite driving the stroke. That is Material's indeterminate circular progress, not a placeholder.

So the honest version of this finding is narrower than "make your skeletons shimmer". Google's answer to waiting is usually a spinner over a settled layout, or content that streams in. The part of this that is not a judgment call: #f9fafb on #ffffff is a 1.03:1 skeleton, which is invisible no matter whose pattern you follow. Fix the colour first and treat the shimmer as optional.

What Google does not do, that you already correctly do not do

Worth stating so you do not change these while changing everything else.

  • Shadows on content. blog.google, aistudio, news.google and labs rendered zero box-shadows between them. Your work and post pages also render zero. That is correct and it is the single most commonly missed thing about Google's look.
  • Borders instead of fills. Google separates with hairlines and tonal surfaces, not outlined boxes stacked on outlined boxes. So do you.
  • A narrow ink ramp. Your homepage uses three text colours. That discipline is right.
  • A reading measure that is actually correct. 558px at roughly 60 characters is in the ideal band. DeepMind's own max-width-inline token is 624px.

Where Google's elevation does appear, it is worth copying exactly. ai.google.dev renders rgba(60,64,67,0.3) 0 1px 2px, rgba(60,64,67,0.15) 0 2px 6px 2px. The shadow colour is #3c4043, Google's grey-800, not black. Your elevation tokens are Tailwind's default black-based ramp. If you ever add a menu or a dialog to the public side, use the grey.

What to do, in order

Ordered by change in feel per hour of work. The first group is most of the difference.

Pass one: the values that are simply wrong

Token edits and a handful of global rules. No component rewrites. This is where the site stops looking approximately Google and starts looking Google.

  1. Remove letter-spacing: 0.5px from body. Correct the nine tracking values in the M3 type scale. Finding 1 and 2.
  2. Darken --md-sys-color-outline so a boundary is visible, and demote the current value to outline-variant. Finding 5.
  3. Add the global :focus-visible rule. Finding 8.
  4. Change the skeleton bar colour from bg-muted to the grey-100 equivalent and swap the pulse for a shimmer. Finding 10.
  5. Add Google Sans Text, point body and label roles at it. Finding 3.

Pass two: the system corrections

  1. Pin the root at 16px and move fluidity onto display type only. This one touches layout, so it wants checking at 320, 390, 768, 1024, 1440 and 1920. Finding 4.
  2. Article body to 16px / 26px in #1f1f1f. Finding 6.
  3. Replace the motion tokens with the two-axis set, and split usage: effects for colour, spatial for anything that moves. Finding 9.
  4. Promote the state layer out of .md-btn into a utility, and apply it to feed rows, work blocks and chips. Finding 7.
  5. Give buttons a 40px height and a real variant set. Spec section.

Pass three: the distinctive layer

This is the part that stops it reading as a good Material clone and starts it reading as something with a point of view. It is also the part most likely to go wrong, so it goes last.

  1. Drive the variable axes: ROND 100 on display, wdth 92 on body, light display weights around 320 to 340. Requires the font link change first. Finding 12.
  2. Add the radius response on hover for cards and feed rows. Finding 7 and 11.
  3. Extend the shape scale, raise content card radius, add the directional variants. Finding 11.
  4. Add loading.tsx to the five routes that have none. Finding 10.

Route the type scale through utilities at some point

Not urgent and not glamorous, but 112 arbitrary sizes is the reason every one of these passes is more work than it should be. Once text-[0.8125rem] is text-label-lg in 42 places, the next time you want to change your mind it is one line rather than forty-two.

Two things I am not confident about

Separated out so they do not get mixed in with the measured claims.

  • Whether Google uses skeletons at all. Four throttled loads produced one spinner and no skeletons, which tells me Google's default is a progress indicator rather than a placeholder, but four pages is not a survey and a detector can miss things. The claim I stand behind is narrower: your skeleton at 1.03:1 against the page is invisible, and that is true regardless of what Google does. Note also that your skeletons are structurally better than a spinner would be, since they hold the real layout. Do not throw them away on the strength of this.
  • The fluid root. I am confident that reading text should not scale with the window, because every text-heavy Google property pins it. I am less confident that your specific clamp is wrong for your layout, because it was written to solve a real problem you had measured. If you disagree with this one, the rest of the report survives without it.
